Chess is a good way to meet people for one simple reason. You sit across from a stranger for half an hour, and nobody expects small talk. The game does the talking. By the second game you know their name. By the third week you know their favourite opening and how they take their coffee.

Toronto has more chess than you'd guess. Some of it happens in cafés, some in a university reading room, some on picnic tables in a park, and some at rated club nights where people track every point. Here's where to go, sorted by the kind of player you are. Every group below comes from [the directory's chess page](/chess/).

## If you just want a casual game

No membership, no rating, no sign-up form. You bring yourself, and maybe a board.

- **[Hart House Chess Club](/chess/#hart-house-chess-club)** opens the Reading Room at 7 Hart House Circle for casual play every Friday from 4pm to 11pm, and drop-in play is free. It's the University of Toronto's club, running since 1895, but community members are welcome.
- **[Etobicoke Chess Club](/chess/#etobicoke-chess-club)** takes over the Second Cup at 265 Wincott Drive on Friday nights from 6pm to 9pm. Free, no membership, and the club's own line is "Play for fun, Play for real, Play for free."

If you've been searching for a chess café in Toronto, the Etobicoke Friday night is the one to try first.

## If you want a club with a social side

A weekly club gives you the same faces, which is how a game becomes a friendship.

- **[Beaches Chess Club](/chess/#beaches-chess-club)** is free and volunteer-run, meeting Sundays from 2pm to 5pm. In winter it's indoors at Beaches Sandbox, 2181 Queen Street East; in summer it moves to the Leuty Boathouse in Kew Gardens. Casual games, blitz tournaments and instructional workshops.
- **[Willowdale Chess Club](/chess/#willowdale-chess-club)** has been running in North York for more than 40 years. Tuesday regular play runs 6pm to 9pm at Earl Bales Community Centre, 4169 Bathurst Street, with a Saturday drop-in at Edithvale Community Centre. It costs $10 a year, which might be the best deal in this post.

## If you want to get better

Playing the same people every week is how you improve. It's also how you make friends, which is a nice side effect.

- **[Annex Chess Club](/chess/#annex-chess-club)** meets Mondays from 7pm to 11pm at 918 Bathurst Street. There's casual play plus a weekly Swiss tournament, and the club also runs the Toronto Open, Toronto Closed and Toronto Blitz, and organizes the Toronto Women's Chess Club.
- **[Hart House Chess Club](/chess/#hart-house-chess-club)** runs weekly lessons for novices, taught by the club's top players.
- **[Chess Institute of Canada](/chess/#chess-institute-of-canada)** is a Toronto charity teaching chess through school clubs, camps and classes for kids and seniors. Useful if you want real structure, or if your kid has started beating you.

## If you want rated games and tournaments

- **[Scarborough Chess Club](/chess/#scarborough-chess-club)** has run since 1960 and holds CFC- and FIDE-rated Swiss tournaments on Thursdays from 6:30pm to 11pm at Birkdale Community Centre, 1299 Ellesmere Road. There are sections for every strength, and boards, pieces and clocks are provided. Tournaments run September to June.
- **[Mississauga Chess Club](/chess/#mississauga-chess-club)** bills itself as Canada's biggest chess club, with players from beginner to master. Adult tournament nights run Wednesdays from 6:30pm to 10:30pm at Erindale United Church, 1444 Dundas Crescent, with casual games alongside.
- **[Greater Toronto Chess League](/chess/#greater-toronto-chess-league)** is the directory of GTA clubs, with locations and schedules for every member club plus tournament listings. Use it to find a rated club near you if none of the above fits your week.

## What about park chess?

Toronto has this one covered. **[Chess in the Park](/chess/#chess-in-the-park)** plays outdoors through the summer and moves indoors when it gets cold, exploring the city's parks and chess tables. It's free, and it meets on weekends — usually Saturday or Sunday, with the location changing weekly, so check the group's Facebook page before you go.

## How to stick with it

Chess clubs reward the same rule as every other group in this city: go three times before you decide. The first night you're the new face. The second night someone remembers your last game. The third night you're a regular, and someone asks you for a rematch. That rule is the core of my guide to [making friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/).

One more tip: lose gracefully and ask questions. Most chess players love to explain what you did wrong. Let them. It's the fastest way into a conversation.
