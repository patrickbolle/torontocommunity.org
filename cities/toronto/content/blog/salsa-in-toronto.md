Salsa in Toronto isn't only a summer thing. The big free street festival is the loudest introduction, but the weekly social is where the same faces start to look familiar.

Where the scene actually meets, and what to expect if you're going for the first time.

## What it is

The anchor is the **Sunday Salsa Bachata Social** at [Dovercourt House](/dance/#dovercourt-house), run by Toronto Dance Fridays. It's the closest thing the city has to a standing Sunday appointment: a salsa lesson, a bachata lesson, two rooms of dancing, and a crowd that runs from total beginners to people who have been at it for years.

The salsa lesson at 6pm is taught by [UnitedSalseros](/dance/#unitedsalseros) in three levels, beginner, intermediate and advanced, so you're not dropped in with people far ahead of you. A separate bachata lesson starts at 6:45pm. At 7:30 the lessons stop and the social dancing takes over: salsa on one floor from 7:30, bachata on the other from 8, each with its own DJ, until 11:30pm. Some Sundays add performances from local dance companies and visiting instructors.

It costs $15. If you were at the Friday social and kept your wristband, or you pre-register on the Friday, it's $5 off. The venue is a short walk from Ossington Station, which makes it an easy solo trip.

## When and where

- **Sunday Salsa Bachata Social:** Every Sunday at Dovercourt House, 805 Dovercourt Rd. Lessons 6-7:30pm, dancing until 11:30pm. $15.
- **TD Salsa on St. Clair:** July 11-12, 2026, on St. Clair Avenue West at Winona Dr. Free. Saturday 11am, 9:30pm, Sunday 11am, 8:30pm.
- **Lula Lounge:** Fridays and Saturdays at 1585 Dundas St W. Beginner lesson at 9pm, live band at 10pm. Cover charge.

The 2026 festival is over. Next year's dates go up on the [official TD Salsa on St. Clair page](https://salsaintoronto.com/festivals/salsa-on-st-clair/), check it before you plan around the weekend. (Checked October 2026.)

## What to expect as a first-timer

You don't need experience or a partner.

Come for the lesson, not after it. At Dovercourt the salsa lesson starts at 6pm and the bachata lesson at 6:45, and both are pitched at beginners as well as people who already dance. The lessons are drop-in and multi-level, so you can turn up alone and join in. Then stay for the social dancing, that's the part where you actually meet people. The two rooms mean you can pick salsa or bachata depending on what clicked.

Wear shoes you can turn in. A smooth sole is better than a grippy running shoe, and you'll be on your feet for hours. Bring cash for the cover.

My advice: go alone if you have to. Stay for the social even if you feel clumsy. And go back the next Sunday, the second week is when faces start to look familiar.

## Other places to dance in Toronto

The Sunday social runs all year, but it isn't the only room in the city. These are all from [the directory's dance page](/dance/):

- **[Lula Lounge](/dance/#lula-lounge)** is Toronto's live Latin room: Cuban Fridays and Salsa Saturdays, with a 30-minute beginner lesson at 9pm before the band.
- **[Steps Dance Studio](/dance/#steps-dance-studio)** teaches salsa and bachata at every level, plus cha cha and Latin hustle, at 819 Yonge St.
- **[Toronto Dance Salsa](/dance/#toronto-dance-salsa)** runs Level 1 through advanced lessons and regular parties and socials, with a big student community.
- **[UnitedSalseros](/dance/#unitedsalseros)** is the long-running school behind the salsa lesson at the Sunday social.
- **[Soul2Sole Latin Dance Co.](/dance/#soul2sole-latin-dance-co.)** runs salsa and bachata classes and socials across the GTA. Mississauga, Richmond Hill, Vaughan and Toronto.
- **[Toronto Swing Dance Society](/dance/#toronto-swing-dance-society-(tsds))** and **[Swing Bandits Dance](/dance/#swing-bandits-dance)** are where to go if swing turns out to be more your thing.
