Every December, a lot of us get the same feeling. We want to help. We want to do something with our hands that isn't shopping. And we'd like some company while we do it.

Volunteering gives you all three. Here's where to start in Toronto this holiday season, and how to sign up in time. Most of the groups below are on [the directory's volunteer page](/volunteer/), and I checked each organization's own site in November 2026 for the details.

## Sign up early

This is the one tip that matters most. Most places need you to apply, attend an orientation or pass a check before your first shift. That takes time. December is also when everyone else wants to help.

This month alone, [Scott Mission](/volunteer/#scott-mission) — a downtown Toronto charity founded in 1941 — says plainly on its volunteer page: "The Christmas season has been filled." [Toronto Humane Society](/volunteer/#toronto-humane-society) has paused its volunteer application form because of the volume already in the queue.

So apply now, even if you can only give a few hours in the new year. Holiday help is lovely. January help is just as needed.

## Food banks

**[Daily Bread Food Bank](/volunteer/#daily-bread-food-bank)** is the first place most people think of, and one of the biggest names in the city's food-bank network. Volunteers sort and repack donated food, pick orders for member agencies, prep soups and casseroles in the kitchen, or help at the welcome centre. It all happens at the warehouse at 191 New Toronto Street, in South Etobicoke.

A few things to know before you apply:

- The minimum age is typically 18. Some youth and student roles differ, and high-schoolers can earn community hours by running a food drive through its Take Action Project.
- You register on the food bank's Volunteer Hub and book a short online info session first.
- Most shifts are about three hours, on weekday mornings and afternoons.
- Groups are welcome, including community groups, corporate teams and schools from grades 6 to 12.

**[Second Harvest](/volunteer/#second-harvest)** is Canada's largest food-rescue organization, redirecting surplus food from grocers and restaurants to nonprofits across the GTA. Individual volunteers can train as a Food Sort Team Leader, with three-hour shifts Tuesday to Thursday. Groups of 5 to 50 can book a food sort of their own.

**[The Stop Community Food Centre](/volunteer/#the-stop-community-food-centre)** on Davenport Road runs a food bank, drop-in meals, community kitchens, a farmers' market and urban-agriculture programs. Volunteers help keep all of it going — a warm, hands-on place where neighbours cook and eat together rather than just pick up a hamper.

## Holiday meals and cold-night shifts

Not every holiday shift happens in a warehouse. Some of the most useful ones happen in a kitchen or on a van, on the coldest evenings of the year.

**[Scott Mission](/volunteer/#scott-mission)** has served people in downtown Toronto since 1941 — meals, shelter, groceries and clothing — with its kitchen and food bank at 502 Spadina Avenue. Volunteers work in the reheat kitchen serving sit-down and takeout meals, in the food and clothing bank, or on evening shelter meals at 346 Spadina. The catch is the one above: this year's Christmas spots are already full. The application is a real process — online form, two references, screening and an orientation — so put your name in now for January.

**[Ve'ahavta](/volunteer/#ve%27ahavta)** sends its Mobile Jewish Response to Homelessness van out on weekday evenings with warm food and supplies. Van shifts are five hours, Monday to Friday, and teens 12 and up can ride along with a guardian. If evenings don't work, the kitchen and warehouse take two- to three-hour weekday shifts. There's also Make-a-Blanket, a no-sew blanket project for the cold winter months that you can do with a group at home.

**[Fred Victor](/volunteer/#fred-victor)** works on housing, shelter, health and food security, mostly in the city's east end. Individual volunteers attend an orientation and commit to at least a month, and most roles run weekdays from 8am to 3pm.

A warm kitchen, a cold night, and a steady line of people who need something hot. That's a good shift.

## Animals

**[Toronto Humane Society](/volunteer/#toronto-humane-society)** is for animal lovers. Its 400-plus volunteers handle feline care, canine care, special species care, building tours and the Pet Parent Support Network. You need to be over 18, and most roles ask for a weekly shift over at least six months.

It's also the clearest lesson in applying early. The application form is temporarily closed while the team works through the queue, so check the page for the next intake. In the meantime, the City of Toronto's animal-fostering program takes volunteers who can open their homes to kittens, cats and dogs temporarily.

## Hands-on and outdoors

**[Habitat for Humanity GTA](/volunteer/#habitat-for-humanity-gta)** builds affordable homes across the region and runs the ReStores that fund the work. Volunteers must be 16 or older. You can join a build-site crew, help customers and sort donations at a ReStore, or bring a team for a one-time day.

**[Evergreen Brick Works](/volunteer/#venues-%26-resources)** is a restored Don Valley brick factory turned community hub. Public-market volunteers staff activations in every season, including the Winter Village, and Garden Circle sessions run day and evening through the week — compost, invasive-plant removal, and a lot of good conversation.

**[Toronto Nature Stewards](/volunteer/#venues-%26-resources)** works at 53 public sites across the city, removing harmful plants, planting natives, picking up trash and teaching neighbours about the ecosystems on their doorstep. A winter workday is a fine way to clear your head between holiday dinners.

**[High Park Nature Centre](/volunteer/#venues-%26-resources)** takes volunteers for its Winter Break Camp Counsellor Assistant role over the school holidays, plus nature-club assistants and community-science projects through the year. Volunteers must be 15-plus and attend an orientation.

**[Toronto and Region Conservation Authority](/volunteer/#toronto-and-region-conservation-authority)** runs planting days and invasive-species pulls at parks and conservation areas across the GTA. Its Conservation Youth Corps is built for high-school community hours, and Girls Can Too gives young women hands-on outdoor experience.

## If you're not sure where to start

Browse a list first. **[Volunteer Toronto](https://www.volunteertoronto.ca/)** is the city's main volunteer board: a free, searchable database of roles with nonprofits across Toronto. It isn't on the directory's volunteer page, but it's the fastest way to see what's open this week.

The **[City of Toronto](/volunteer/#venues-%26-resources)** runs its own programs too, including park stewardship, community recreation, animal fostering, long-term care, and a cultural-events crew that staffs Cavalcade of Lights and New Year's Eve.

And if you're looking for a 2SLGBTQ+ community, **[The 519](/volunteer/#the-519)** at Church and Wellesley has programs, events and advocacy running all year. Its 2026 volunteer recruitment is closed, but the waitlist for 2027 is open now.

## Why it's a good way to meet people

Volunteering is one of the easiest ways to make friends in a new city. You have a job to do, so you don't have to think of small talk. You just pass the boxes, or the plates, and the conversation starts on its own.

The trick is to go back. One holiday shift feels good. A regular shift turns into a crew. If you want more ideas, read my guide on [making friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/).
