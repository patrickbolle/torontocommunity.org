Somewhere between "grab a coffee sometime" and "come to my dinner party" sits the perfect Toronto social format: a seat at a table full of strangers. You show up alone, you eat well, and by dessert you've met five people you'd never have crossed paths with otherwise. No hosting, no small-talk cold start, and you don't need a plus-one.

How dinner-with-strangers and supper clubs work in Toronto, and where to find a seat. Everything here is a real option from [the directory's dinner & supper clubs page](/dinner-supper-clubs/).

## The weekly "dinner with strangers" services

These are the easiest way in, book a seat, get matched, show up.

- **[Timeleft](/dinner-supper-clubs/#timeleft)** matches you with a small group of strangers over dinner, drinks, coffee or a walk. Toronto listings include Wednesday dinners and Monday women-only dinners, CA$15.99 to book a seat, with the venue shared before you go.
- **[Never Eat Alone](/dinner-supper-clubs/#never-eat-alone)** is the low-tech version: a 7,000-plus-member Meetup group that books long restaurant tables, often two tables on the same night so you're seated with new people. You cover your own dinner and any event fee.

**Going alone is the point.** A table of solo bookers bonds faster than one where half the seats came in pairs, and if you freeze at the "so who do I invite?" step, it solves that too.

## Underground supper clubs and pop-ups

If you want the food to be the event:

- **[The Depanneur Supper Clubs](/dinner-supper-clubs/#the-depanneur-supper-clubs)** is the veteran: since 2011, a different guest chef each week cooks a family-style, BYOB dinner for up to 32 at CSI Spadina. The long communal tables do most of the introductions for you.
- **[Gather Supper Club](/dinner-supper-clubs/#gather-supper-club)** seats a small group of strangers in Victoria and Steve's family home off Queen West, a cocktail and canapés, then a three-course family-style dinner with wine and beer, for $120. Conversation prompts handle the awkward part.
- **[Tuba's Secret Dinner Parties](/dinner-supper-clubs/#tuba's-secret-dinner-parties)** is the apartment-scale version: Daniel Tuba D'Souza seats about 20 strangers for six courses in a downtown apartment, with menus drawn from Korea, Sichuan, Japan and Portugal. He charges roughly what the food costs; book through his TikTok.
- **[Oui Aïa](/dinner-supper-clubs/#oui-a%C3%AFa)** puts on a monthly supper at a private downtown address revealed after you buy a ticket, eight to 10 tapas-style dishes, a cocktail, and a new menu every time.

Also worth a look: [Rhyz Supper Club](/dinner-supper-clubs/#rhyz-supper-club)'s wine-paired dinners in Etobicoke, the [Saturday Spice Series](/dinner-supper-clubs/#saturday-spice-series), and [Eat Out Supper Club](/dinner-supper-clubs/#eat-out-supper-club)'s roving queer dinners.

## Why dinner works so well for meeting people

Eating together is the oldest social technology there is. A shared table gives you a built-in reason to talk, a natural rhythm (courses!), and a fixed end time so there's no awkward "when do we leave?" A dinner with strangers is one of the highest-yield social hours in the city, which is why it's near the top of our guide to [making friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/) and our list of [things to do alone in Toronto](/blog/things-to-do-alone-in-toronto/).

One tip: say yes to the after-plan. When someone at the table says "a few of us are getting a drink", that's the actual invitation. Take it.
