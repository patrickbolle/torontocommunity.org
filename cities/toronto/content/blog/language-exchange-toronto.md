Language exchange might be the most underrated way to build a social life in Toronto. The whole format is *designed* around talking to strangers, patiently, kindly, in two languages, which makes it one of the warmest, most newcomer-friendly rooms in the city. You come to practise a language; you leave with a standing Wednesday and a few new friends.

Where to practise in Toronto, by language, and how to get the most out of it. All of these are listed on [the directory's language exchange page](/language-exchange/).

## By language

- **Spanish & English**, **[TorontoBabel](/language-exchange/#torontobabel)** is the big, easy one: a multi-language social every Wednesday, 7-11pm, at Madison Avenue Pub (14 Madison Ave), with native speakers of Spanish, French, Korean, Japanese, Mandarin and more. It's a bar night with a $5 entry donation. **[Spanglish Toronto](/language-exchange/#spanglish-toronto)** is a more casual Spanish/English exchange at venues around the city, and **[Say hello, Di hola](/language-exchange/#say-hello%2C-di-hola)** runs a Latino-Canadian conversation night.
- **Mandarin & English**, **[UTME](/language-exchange/#university-of-toronto-mandarin-exchange-club-%28utme%29)** is U of T's Mandarin Exchange Club, with regular sessions for beginners, heritage speakers and fluent speakers on the St George campus. **[Chinese Chat Cafe](/language-exchange/#chinese-chat-cafe-%28tmu%29)** runs Mandarin conversation tables through Toronto Metropolitan University's languages department.
- **Japanese & English**, **[TJEX](/language-exchange/#toronto-japanese-english-exchange-%28tjex%29)** is the city's most structured exchange: every Saturday, 5:30-7pm at the Atrium food court (595 Bay St, by Dundas station), you're placed in a small group by level for 45 minutes of Japanese and 45 minutes of English. RSVP ahead, it fills up, and there's an optional dinner after.
- **French & English**, the **[Downtown Toronto French Conversation Exchange](/language-exchange/#downtown-toronto-french-conversation-exchange)** is informal and welcomes all levels, though a roughly conversational level helps. For something more structured, **[Alliance Française Toronto](/language-exchange/#alliance-fran%C3%A7aise-toronto)** has taught French here since 1902, with courses and cultural events across five GTA campuses from its Spadina Road home.
- **German & English**, the **[Greater Toronto German Club](/language-exchange/#greater-toronto-german-club)** has run monthly events for years: a Stammtisch on the third Wednesday, a Saturday brunch on the third Saturday, pub nights in Mississauga and Oakville, and conversation evenings for learners.
- **Italian & English**, **[Toronto Italian](/language-exchange/#toronto-italian)** meets at Italian restaurants around the city to talk, eat, and argue about films and novels in Italian.
- **Korean & English**, the directory's long-running Say Kimchi exchange no longer has a live Meetup page, so the best bet right now is TorontoBabel's Wednesday social, where native Korean speakers are regulars.
- **English conversation**, for newcomers practising English, the **[Toronto Public Library English Conversation Circle](/language-exchange/#toronto-public-library-english-conversation-circle)** is free and drop-in at branches across the city.

## How to get the most out of a language exchange

**Go alone, and go regularly.** Like every good social format in Toronto, the value compounds, the second visit someone remembers you, the third you're a regular. (More on that in our guide to [making friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/).)

**Trade fairly.** The unwritten rule of exchange is half your time in each language. TJEX builds it into the schedule, 45 minutes each way, and that's a good model anywhere: give as much patience as you'd like to receive when it's your turn to stumble.

**It's fine to be a beginner.** Most groups welcome all levels; TJEX sorts you into a group at your level, and TorontoBabel will point you to native speakers who are learning what you already know. Nobody's judging your accent; they're too busy worrying about theirs.

If you'd rather meet people without the language angle, these rooms still count as some of the [best solo-friendly things to do in the city](/blog/things-to-do-alone-in-toronto/), you always have something to talk about.

Looking for a specific language not listed? New groups join most weeks, [browse the full language exchange list](/language-exchange/), or [add one you know about](/submit/).
