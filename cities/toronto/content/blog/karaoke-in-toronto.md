Karaoke is a strange gift. Almost nobody is good at it, and that's the point. You stand up, you get it a bit wrong, and a room of strangers cheers anyway. Not many places in a city let you be bad at something in public and still feel welcome.

Toronto has a few kinds of karaoke. There are private rooms where only your friends can hear you. There are bars with a weekly night and a crowd. And there are late-night rooms that keep going until last call. How to pick, sorted by how brave you feel. All of these come from [the directory's karaoke page](/karaoke/).

## Private karaoke rooms

If you want to sing without an audience of strangers, book a room. You get your own space, your own queue and nobody else's birthday party.

**[Bar+ Karaoke Lounge](/karaoke/#bar+-karaoke-lounge)** has private rooms at 360 Yonge Street, near Dundas. Song selection runs through a tablet and app, the library is multilingual, and there's a photobooth for the group photo. Rooms fit small groups up to parties of 30, so it works well for a birthday or a team night out.

**[Boom Karaoke](/karaoke/#boom-karaoke)** is the sleek basement room at 577 Yonge Street in Church-Wellesley. It has private rooms, 150,000+ songs and a proper cocktail list, and it's built for groups who want the mic to themselves.

**[SU Karaoke Bar](/karaoke/#su-karaoke-bar)** at 649 Yonge Street, near Church, keeps private rooms open late seven days a week. The songbook is multilingual and the drink menu is full, which makes it an easy, unstuffy pick for a group night.

## Karaoke bars and weekly nights

A private room is safe. A bar is where the magic happens. Someone you've never met belts out a power ballad, and suddenly the whole room is singing along.

**[Toronto Karaoke Meetup Group](/karaoke/#toronto-karaoke-meetup-group)** is the easiest way in if you're showing up alone. Host KJ Sharon reserves a table near the stage, makes introductions and runs icebreakers. Regulars sing Thursdays at The Irish Rose (1095 Weston Road) and Fridays at Scruffy Murphy's (225 The East Mall, Etobicoke), both at 9pm. The group is big and friendly, and nobody minds a first-timer.

**[Amuse Bouche Karaoke at Three Dollar Bill](/karaoke/#amuse-bouche-karaoke-at-three-dollar-bill)** is Sunday-night queer karaoke at 1592 Queen Street West. Guest hosts keep it loose, birthday groups and duets are part of the ritual, and the room treats first-timers like regulars. Sunday is also a kind slot for a first try. Nobody expects much from a Sunday.

**[Cassette Karaoke](/karaoke/#cassette-karaoke)** runs every Saturday at 8pm in the Gladstone House bar at 1214 Queen Street West. It's hosted by Question Everything, there's no cover, and the crowd knows every word. Once a month the mic goes live-band with Good Enough Live Karaoke.

**[Dina's Tavern Karaoke](/karaoke/#dina's-tavern-karaoke)** is the Spadina tavern at 486 Spadina Avenue with karaoke several nights a week: Thursdays with Ivan Rivers, live-band karaoke and guest-hosted parties. Affordable drinks, low attitude and plenty of regulars.

**[Saturday Karaoke at The Lodge](/karaoke/#saturday-karaoke-at-the-lodge)** is free, upstairs at O'Grady's at 518 Church Street, and runs from 10pm to 2am. Brayo Bryans hosts a queer-friendly room with big anthems and a proper after-party crowd. It's the late option when the rest of the city is closing.

## If you're shy about singing

Plenty of people love karaoke and dread the microphone. That's fine. You have options.

Start in a private room with two or three friends. Bar+, Boom and SU all let you sing in front of people you already know, and once you've done that, singing in front of strangers feels much less dramatic.

If you want to try a real stage, pick the earliest, lowest-stakes slot. Cassette's Saturday night starts at 8pm with no cover, the room is there to sing along, and you can slip in and out without ceremony. Or join the Toronto Karaoke Meetup Group, where KJ Sharon reserves a table and runs icebreakers, so you're not walking in on your own.

Toronto's karaoke is mostly a nighttime thing. There aren't many daytime sing-alongs, so an early Saturday night is the closest thing to a warm-up.

## Karaoke as a way to meet people

Karaoke is secretly a great way to make friends. Everyone in the room has already agreed to look a bit silly, so the usual walls come down fast. You clap for a stranger's song, they clap for yours, and now you have something to talk about.

The trick is the same as with any group: go back. A weekly night like Thursdays at The Irish Rose with the Meetup group, or Sundays at Three Dollar Bill, works best for this. The first time, you're a stranger with a song slip. By the third, the regulars know your song.
