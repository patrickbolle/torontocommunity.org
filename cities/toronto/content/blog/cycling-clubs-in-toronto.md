Cycling in Toronto is almost unfair — the Don Valley ravines thread right through the city, the Martin Goodman Trail runs for kilometres along the lake, and quiet country roads start barely an hour north. What's harder to find is the *group*: people to ride with who match your pace and don't make you feel like you showed up to the wrong party. Good news — there's a club for every kind of rider here.

Here's how to find yours, by riding style. Every club below is real and active, from [the directory's cycling page](/cycling/).

## If you just want the community (no pressure)

- **[Toronto Cruisers](/cycling/#toronto-cruisers)** calls it right: lights and music, a hop-on hop-off cruise that keeps the group together and changes route every week. No membership, no RSVP, just show up at Bloor and Huron on a Wednesday.
- **[Etobicoke Cycling Club](/cycling/#etobicoke-cycling-club)** rides almost every day at a range of paces, with a no-drop policy and a coffee stop on every ride. Their early-season intro program exists to settle new riders in.
- **[Bikechain](/cycling/#bikechain)** runs free, beginner-friendly Tuesday-evening rides from the University of Toronto bike shop — a talking pace, no one left behind.
- **[Urbane Cyclist](/cycling/#urbane-cyclist)**'s Urbane Adventure Club puts on easy, no-drop mixed-terrain rides around the city, announced on Instagram.
- **[Bateman's Bicycle Company](/cycling/#bateman's-bicycle-company)** splits its free Thursday-evening Don Valley mountain bike rides into slow, medium and fast groups, so you can pick the one that fits.

## If you ride road and want distance or training

- **[Toronto Bicycling Network](/cycling/#toronto-bicycling-network)** is the GTA's largest recreational club, volunteer-run, with urban and country rides almost every day of the season — $50 a year, plus a social calendar off the bike.
- **[Dark Horse Flyers](/cycling/#dark-horse-flyers)** — 300 members, riding since 2007, with six levels so newcomers and racers each find their group. Saturday routes stretch out through the season.
- **[Beaches Cycling Club](/cycling/#beaches-cycling-club)** has been a safety-first, social Beaches club since 2007 — road, gravel, mountain and triathlon riders all welcome, with early-morning rides and coffee stops.
- **[D'Ornellas Cycling Club](/cycling/#d'ornellas-cycling-club)** — founded by four-time Olympian Eon D'Ornellas, running skills-focused rides out of Scarborough and Richmond Hill.
- **[Midweek Cycling Club](/cycling/#midweek-cycling-club)** is the learn-to-race on-ramp: coached training rides, a weekly crit series and youth programs.
- **[Newmarket Eagles](/cycling/#newmarket-eagles-cycling-club)** takes York Region riders out on quiet rural roads, with time trials and a race team for the competitive.

## If you ride gravel — or want your specific crowd

- **[LapDogs Cycling Club](/cycling/#lapdogs-cycling-club)** covers road, mountain, cyclocross, gravel and track, and women and gender-diverse riders new to the club can request a first-ride chaperone.
- **[Saddle Sisters](/cycling/#saddle-sisters)** runs no-drop morning rides and bikepacking trips for women, trans, femme and non-binary riders.
- **[Queer Bike Club TO](/cycling/#queer-bike-club-to)** holds biweekly queer rides on mixed terrain, open to all levels and paced to the slowest rider.
- **[Trailblazers Tandem Cycling Club](/cycling/#trailblazers-tandem-cycling-club)** has been pairing blind, deaf-blind and partially sighted riders with sighted volunteer captains since 1986 — one of the most welcoming communities in Toronto cycling.
- **[Randonneurs Ontario — Toronto Chapter](/cycling/#randonneurs-ontario-%25E2%2580%2594-toronto-chapter)** is the long-distance crowd: self-supported brevets from 200 km, starting in Leslieville, Mimico and High Park.

## Getting the most out of a group ride

**Match the pace honestly.** Every club above lists its style — TBN's ride classifications and Dark Horse's six levels exist precisely so you don't get shelled off the back of a race pace.

**The coffee stop is the point.** Group rides bond over the mid-ride espresso, not the KOM. Etobicoke builds one into every ride; Beaches finishes its mornings the same way. Show up for that part.

**Go weekly, not once.** Like every good way to [make friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/), cycling clubs work by repetition — the same Wednesday cruise, the same Saturday route, the same faces, until they're your people.
