Most people start meditating alone, at home, with an app. That works for a while. Sitting quietly in a room with other people is different: a strange kind of company, but company. Toronto has a good range of these rooms, Buddhist and otherwise, and a few are free. Every group below is on [the directory's meditation page](/mindfulness-meditation/).

## If you want to try it for free

**[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** runs free introductory classes across the GTA, from downtown and the Annex to Etobicoke and King City. Sessions use breathing, mantra and group kirtan, and the calendar adds kirtans and vegetarian feasts.

**[Brahma Kumaris Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#brahma-kumaris-toronto)** offers every program free at Inspiration House, 3000 Islington Ave in North York. The World Meditation Hour on the third Sunday needs no experience.

**[Kadampa Meditation Centre Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#kadampa-meditation-centre-canada)** holds a free Meditate for World Peace class alongside its $15 drop-ins at 631 Crawford St, near Bloor and Christie. The **[Theravada Buddhist Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** charges no fees either; its Sunday mornings run on dana, voluntary giving.

## If you're a complete beginner

Nobody expects you to know what you're doing. These groups say so out loud.

**[Toronto Zen Centre](/mindfulness-meditation/#toronto-zen-centre)** is the most structured way in. Founded in 1967, it sits at 33 High Park Gardens. Newcomers attend a half-day Introduction to Zen workshop covering postures and zazen, then get a month of trial membership to try the daily schedule.

**[Kadampa](/mindfulness-meditation/#kadampa-meditation-centre-canada)** runs drop-in classes Tuesday to Thursday, describes them as suitable for all levels, and includes guided meditations. They cost $15; pre-booking is encouraged, though last-minute drop-ins are welcome.

**[Theravada](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** meets Sundays 10am to noon at the Carrot Common, 348 Danforth Ave, Room 212: chanting, a 45-minute silent sit, a reading or talk, and discussion. The only requirement is sitting quietly.

**[Snow Lion Meditation Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#snow-lion-meditation-community)** has a before-your-first-class guide and meets Friday 7 to 8:30pm at 662 Pape Ave and online. The teacher works in both secular and Buddhist frames.

## If you want a Buddhist sangha

A sangha is a community that practises together. For a lot of people, that's the point.

**[Mindfulness Practice Community of Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#mindfulness-practice-community-of-toronto)** has practised in Thich Nhat Hanh's Plum Village tradition since 1997. It's interfaith, meets Saturday mornings at Roncesvalles United Church, 214 Wright Ave, and holds weekly online sittings. Friends can drop in any time.

**[Theravada Buddhist Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** is lay-led and follows the Ajahn Chah forest tradition. It's the same Sunday-morning format every week, which makes it easy to become a regular.

**[DDM Toronto Centre](/mindfulness-meditation/#ddm-toronto-centre)** is a Chan community in the Dharma Drum lineage, in Scarborough since 1998. At 1025 McNicoll Ave it runs meditation, chanting and study groups, including a Wednesday evening sit with Eight Form Moving Meditation.

**[Nalandabodhi Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#nalandabodhi-toronto)** is a Tibetan centre in the Nyingma tradition, at 1159 Dundas St E, Unit 153 in Leslieville. It offers meditation, philosophy classes and visiting teachers, in person and on Zoom.

## If you'd rather try another tradition

Not everyone wants a Buddhist frame.

**[Heartfulness Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#heartfulness-toronto)** is part of the Sahaj Marg tradition, with a large centre at 1337 Matheson Blvd E in Mississauga. Group sittings are Wednesday at 7:30pm and Sunday at 9am, with individual sittings Saturday afternoons.

**[Tergar Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#tergar-toronto)** follows Mingyur Rinpoche's Joy of Living program. It meets in hybrid sessions on the 2nd and 4th Monday, 7 to 8:30pm, at the Centering Space, 59 Cambridge Ave near Broadview, with guided and unguided meditation plus discussion.

**[Snow Lion](/mindfulness-meditation/#snow-lion-meditation-community)** is the secular-leaning option: formerly Toronto Mindfulness Community, running since 2006, and also teaching MBSR. **[Brahma Kumaris](/mindfulness-meditation/#brahma-kumaris-toronto)** and **[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** above are the other non-Buddhist routes.

## If you want to meet people

Meditation is a gentle way into a group. You don't have to talk much. You just have to show up.

**[Consciousness Explorers Club](/mindfulness-meditation/#consciousness-explorers-club)** is the easiest room to walk into alone: around 700 members, Mondays at 7:30pm at St Stephen-in-the-Fields, 365 College St near Kensington Market. It's pluralist, welcomes absolute beginners, and runs courses, retreats and social events alongside the sit.

**[Heartfulness](/mindfulness-meditation/#heartfulness-toronto)** is another big community, and **[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** builds its calendar around kirtans and vegetarian feasts. The chat before and after the sit is where people get to know each other.

## How to stick with one

Go three times before you decide. The first sit, you'll feel stiff and new. By the third, you'll know where to sit and someone will nod hello. That nod is a small thing. It adds up.

Pick a night that already fits: Sundays at Theravada, Mondays at Consciousness Explorers, the 2nd and 4th Monday at Tergar, Fridays at Snow Lion, the third Sunday at Brahma Kumaris. Geography helps too — Zen in High Park and the Mindfulness Practice Community in Roncesvalles for the west end; Theravada on Danforth and Nalandabodhi in Leslieville for the east; DDM in Scarborough, Heartfulness in Mississauga, Brahma Kumaris in North York. Choose the one you can reach on a dark Tuesday in February.

Check the calendar before a holiday weekend, and note how each handles money: most are drop-in, the Zen Centre needs its workshop first, and Theravada runs on donation.
