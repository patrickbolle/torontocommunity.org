Most people start meditating alone, at home, with an app. That works for a while. But sitting quietly in a room with other people is different. It's a strange kind of company. It's still company.

Toronto has a good range of these rooms. Some are Buddhist. Some come from other traditions. A few are free. Here's how to find one, sorted by what you're after. Every group below comes from [the directory's meditation page](/mindfulness-meditation/).

## If you want to try it for free

**[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** runs free introductory classes across the GTA, from downtown and the Annex to Etobicoke and King City. Sessions use breathing, mantra and group kirtan, and the wider calendar adds kirtans and vegetarian feasts. It's a good first taste if you're curious about a practice with some sound to it.

**[Brahma Kumaris Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#brahma-kumaris-toronto)** offers every program free of charge at Inspiration House, 3000 Islington Ave in North York: courses, talks, workshops and group meditations. The World Meditation Hour, on the third Sunday of each month, is open to anyone and needs no experience. The organization also has centres in Brampton, Burlington, Mississauga and Scarborough.

**[Kadampa Meditation Centre Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#kadampa-meditation-centre-canada)** holds a free Meditate for World Peace class alongside its regular drop-ins, which are $15. It's a low-pressure way to see the temple at 631 Crawford St, near Bloor and Christie.

The **[Theravada Buddhist Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** has no formal fees. Its Sunday gatherings run on dana, the traditional practice of voluntary giving, so you can try a sit before deciding what to contribute.

## If you're a complete beginner

Nobody expects you to know what you're doing. These groups say so out loud.

**[Toronto Zen Centre](/mindfulness-meditation/#toronto-zen-centre)** has the most structured way in. The centre was founded in 1967 and sits in a quiet house at 33 High Park Gardens. Newcomers attend a half-day Introduction to Zen workshop first; it covers postures, zazen and the forms of the practice. After that you get a month of trial membership, which lets you try the daily sitting schedule before committing to anything.

**[Kadampa Meditation Centre Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#kadampa-meditation-centre-canada)** runs drop-in classes on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday and describes them as suitable for all levels. The classes include guided meditations, so you're not left alone with a cushion and a vague instruction. They cost $15, and pre-booking is encouraged, though last-minute drop-ins are welcome.

The **[Theravada Buddhist Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** meets Sundays from 10am to noon at the Carrot Common, 348 Danforth Ave, Room 212. The morning has chanting, a 45-minute silent sit and a reading or recorded talk, followed by discussion and an optional lunch nearby. The only requirement is the willingness to sit quietly.

**[Snow Lion Meditation Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#snow-lion-meditation-community)** runs a before-your-first-class guide on its site, which tells you what to bring and what happens in the room. Its weekly class is Friday from 7 to 8:30pm at 662 Pape Ave and online. The teacher, Theodore Tsaousidis, works in both a secular and a Buddhist frame, so it suits people who aren't sure they want a religious setting.

## If you want a Buddhist sangha

A sangha is a community that practises together. For a lot of people, that's the point.

The **[Mindfulness Practice Community of Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#mindfulness-practice-community-of-toronto)** has been practising in Thich Nhat Hanh's Plum Village tradition since 1997. It's an interfaith group: the meetings are Buddhist, but members are encouraged to keep their own traditions too. In-person sangha meets on Saturday mornings at Roncesvalles United Church, 214 Wright Ave, and there are weekly online sittings. Friends can drop in at any time.

The **[Theravada Buddhist Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#theravada-buddhist-community)** is a lay-led group in the Ajahn Chah forest tradition, connected to Tisarana Forest Monastery and Sati Saraniya Hermitage near Perth, Ontario. It's the same Sunday-morning format every week, which makes it easy to become a regular.

**[DDM Toronto Centre](/mindfulness-meditation/#ddm-toronto-centre)** is a Chan Buddhist community in the Dharma Drum lineage, practising in Scarborough since 1998. Its home at 1025 McNicoll Ave runs regular meditation, chanting and study groups, including a Wednesday evening sit with Eight Form Moving Meditation.

**[Nalandabodhi Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#nalandabodhi-toronto)** is a Tibetan Buddhist centre in the Nyingma tradition, now in a converted warehouse in Leslieville at 1159 Dundas St E, Unit 153. It offers meditation practice, classes in Buddhist philosophy and visiting-teacher events, in person and over Zoom, plus a weekly open practice online.

The Zen Centre and the Theravada community above are sanghas too. The difference is mostly the tradition and the night of the week.

## If you'd rather try another tradition

Not everyone wants a Buddhist frame. The directory doesn't sort groups into "secular" and "religious," so here's what the rest say about themselves.

**[Heartfulness Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#heartfulness-toronto)** is part of the Sahaj Marg tradition. Its centre at 1337 Matheson Blvd E in Mississauga holds group sittings on Wednesday at 7:30pm and Sunday at 9am, with individual sittings on Saturday afternoons. It's a large centre, and the drive is worth it if you live west of the city.

**[Tergar Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#tergar-toronto)** follows Mingyur Rinpoche's Joy of Living program. The group meets in hybrid sessions on the 2nd and 4th Monday of each month from 7 to 8:30pm at the Centering Space, 59 Cambridge Ave, near Broadview Station. Sessions mix guided and unguided meditation with group discussion.

**[Snow Lion Meditation Community](/mindfulness-meditation/#snow-lion-meditation-community)** is the other option if you want mindfulness without doctrine. It was formerly the Toronto Mindfulness Community, has been running since 2006, and also teaches MBSR and advanced mindfulness courses.

The **[Brahma Kumaris](/mindfulness-meditation/#brahma-kumaris-toronto)** and **[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** groups above are the other non-Buddhist routes: Raja Yoga at the first, mantra and kirtan at the second.

## If you want to meet people

Meditation is a gentle way into a group. You don't have to talk much. You just have to show up.

The **[Consciousness Explorers Club](/mindfulness-meditation/#consciousness-explorers-club)** is the easiest room to walk into alone. It has around 700 members and meets every Monday at 7:30pm at St Stephen-in-the-Fields, 365 College St, near Kensington Market. The group is explicitly pluralist, welcomes absolute beginners, and runs courses, retreats and social events alongside the weekly sit. Expect a playful, experimental crowd rather than a silent one.

**[Heartfulness Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#heartfulness-toronto)** is another large community, with group sittings twice a week and individual sittings on Saturdays. A big group means you can stay quiet at the back for a while.

**[Yoga Meditation Canada](/mindfulness-meditation/#yoga-meditation-canada)** builds its calendar around gatherings, kirtans and vegetarian feasts, so there's usually food and conversation after the meditation. The **[Mindfulness Practice Community of Toronto](/mindfulness-meditation/#mindfulness-practice-community-of-toronto)** is smaller and sangha-focused, which means the same faces week to week.

Either way, the chat before and after the sit is where people get to know each other.

## How to stick with one

Go three times before you decide. The first sit, you'll feel stiff and new. By the third, you'll know where to sit and someone will nod hello. That nod is a small thing. It adds up.

Pick a night that already fits your week: Sundays at the Theravada Buddhist Community, Mondays at the Consciousness Explorers Club, the 2nd and 4th Monday at Tergar, Fridays at Snow Lion, the third Sunday at Brahma Kumaris. A fixed night turns a decision into a habit.

Geography helps too. The west end has the Zen Centre in High Park and the Mindfulness Practice Community in Roncesvalles. The east end has the Theravada community on Danforth and Nalandabodhi in Leslieville. Scarborough has DDM, Mississauga has Heartfulness, and North York has Brahma Kumaris. Choose the one you can reach on a dark Tuesday in February, not the one that sounds best on a sunny day in June.

Check the calendar before a holiday weekend, and note how each group handles money: some are drop-in, the Zen Centre needs the introductory workshop first, and the Theravada community runs on donation. None of that is a trap, but it's easier to walk in knowing.
