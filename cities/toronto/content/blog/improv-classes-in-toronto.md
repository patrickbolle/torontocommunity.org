Improv has a reputation problem. People picture a stranger yelling "yes, and" at them while they freeze. I get it. But a beginner class is mostly a room of nervous adults playing games and laughing at their own mistakes. It's one of the friendliest rooms you can walk into.

Toronto is a good city for it. The Second City opened its Toronto theatre in 1973, Bad Dog Theatre has been making long-form since 1982, and SCTV grew out of the Second City's Toronto troupe — the one that counts John Candy, Catherine O'Hara, Eugene Levy and Martin Short among its alumni. Here's where to start, whether you want to take a class or just watch one. Every place below comes from [the directory's improv and comedy page](/improv-comedy/).

## If you want a beginner improv class

**[The Second City Toronto](/improv-comedy/#the-second-city-toronto)** is the big one: 1 York St, nightly mainstage shows and a training centre that takes people from Improv 1 through to its Conservatory. Improv 1 is built around the fundamentals of "yes, and", listening and being present. If you've ever wanted to learn where the professionals learned, this is the room.

**[Bad Dog Theatre Company](/improv-comedy/#bad-dog-theatre-company)** has taught long-form in this city since 1982. Its Foundations program runs 1 through 4 and ends with a certificate; classes are at 392 Spadina Ave, Unit 2. It's the kind of place where the person next to you is also quietly terrified, which helps.

**[SoCap Comedy (The Social Capital)](/improv-comedy/#socap-comedy-(the-social-capital))** on the Danforth runs drop-ins and multi-week courses from its walk-up at 154 Danforth Ave, a short walk from Broadview and Chester stations. The room is not fancy. That's part of the charm.

## If you want something more casual

Not everyone wants to commit to a full class. Some of us want to try it once and see.

**[Bad Dog's beginner drop-in](/improv-comedy/#bad-dog-theatre-company)** is two hours, $25 plus HST, no prerequisites, and capped at ten people. It runs Mondays and Saturdays from 6pm to 8pm. One session is enough to know whether you like it. Confirm the current schedule on Bad Dog's site before you go — class times move around.

**[SoCap's drop-in classes](/improv-comedy/#socap-comedy-(the-social-capital))** are single nights from about $25, with no multi-week commitment. Most people start there and sign up for a course once they're hooked.

**[Improv for New Friends](/improv-comedy/#improv-for-new-friends)** is a two-hour beginner class and social at SoCap, run by Ralph MacLeod, a co-founder of Bad Dog and the Social Capital Theatre. It's built for people who want to meet others: games, scenes and time to mingle afterward. Check the listing for the next date.

## If you're ready for long-form

Short-form improv is the games you've seen on TV. Long-form is longer scenes that build on each other, closer to a play made up on the spot.

**[Bad Dog's long-form program](/improv-comedy/#bad-dog-theatre-company)** picks up after Foundations, with Longform 1 through 3 and advanced classes in shortform, format and scenework. It's a step up once the basics feel comfortable.

**[SoCap's improv courses](/improv-comedy/#socap-comedy-(the-social-capital))** run as multi-week terms with the same group and instructor, from Fundamentals through advanced scenework. Same idea: a room that gets to know each other.

## If you'd rather watch first

Watching is research. It's also a good night out.

- **[Comedy Bar](/improv-comedy/#comedy-bar)** at 945 Bloor St W has two stages and something on seven nights a week: sketch, improv, alternative and stand-up. It's the easiest place to see what the city's indie scene is doing.
- **[SoCap Comedy](/improv-comedy/#socap-comedy-(the-social-capital))** runs improv and comedy shows most nights on the Danforth, so you can watch the form in the same building where you'd take a class.
- **[Sweet Action Theatre](/improv-comedy/#sweet-action-theatre)** is a 55-seat room at 180 Shaw St devoted to clown, improv, sketch and puppetry. The shows are strange in the best way, and the classes are affordable.
- **[Knifefight Theatre](/improv-comedy/#knifefight-theatre)** puts on Kumite Improv, a monthly showdown at The Assembly Theatre, 1479 Queen St W. Six improvisers, three teams of two, four rounds, live scoring and an audience-fuelled final.
- **[The Corner Comedy Club](/improv-comedy/#the-corner-comedy-club)** at 243 Queen St W is a tiny basement room with nightly stand-up and a booked open mic on the last Tuesday of the month — 24 comics, three minutes each. Intimacy is the point.
- **[Backroom Comedy Club](/improv-comedy/#backroom-comedy-club)** near Christie Pits does pro stand-up, happy-hour open mics and a Monday Night Improv where new and established acts try material.

## Why improv is good for making friends

Most ways to meet people involve standing around trying to think of something to say. Improv skips that part. Within ten minutes you're a pirate or a dentist, and so is the person next to you. It's hard to stay a stranger after that.

It also builds confidence in a sneaky way. You practise being wrong in front of people, over and over, and nothing bad happens. That carries into work meetings and first dates. I won't promise it fixes shyness. It does make the fear smaller.

If you want more on that side of things, my guide to [making friends in Toronto](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-toronto/) covers the rest of it.

## How to stick with it

The advice is the same as for any group. Go three times before you decide. The first class you'll feel stiff and self-aware. By the third, you'll know names, and someone will be waiting to play with you. That's the bit that makes it worth it.

You don't need to be funny to start. Most funny people in improv got there by being bad at it first, in public, with friends.
