Writing is lonely work. That's partly the appeal. But at some point most writers want a room of people doing the same strange thing, even if nobody talks for an hour.

Toronto has a good handful of these rooms. Some are silent. Some are social. Some care a lot about dragons. How to find the one that fits, sorted by the kind of writer you are. The directory keeps its running list on [the writing page](/writing/).

## If you need to actually get words down

Plenty of us don't need feedback. We need a time, a place and other people quietly typing so we feel too guilty to scroll.

**[Shut Up & Write Toronto](https://www.meetup.com/shutupandwritetoronto/)** is exactly that. It's free, it has almost 3,000 members, and it runs several sessions a week: West Queen West on Saturday mornings at the Theatre Centre Café/Bar (1115 Queen St W), a Saturday meetup near Bloor-Yonge, a Sunday session in Sheppard-Yonge, and timed sprints online. You show up, you write, you leave with pages.

If you want a door you can close, **[Toronto Writers' Centre](https://www.writerscentre.ca/)** is a quiet writing room at 344 Bloor St W, Suite 401. It has run since 2006, is open to members around the clock, and hosts workshops and drop-in days alongside the desks. Membership is paid, so it suits someone who wants a regular place to work rather than a weekly meetup.

## If you want people to read your work

Sharing work is scarier than writing it. It's also how you get better.

**[Toronto Public Library](https://www.torontopubliclibrary.ca/programs-and-classes/categories/book-clubs-writers-groups.jsp)** runs free writers' groups at branches across the city, and they're the most dependable option in town. The East End Writers' Group meets on the fourth Wednesday of the month, led by editor and author Sharon A. Crawford. Ashdale Writers Group meets bi-weekly at Gerrard/Ashdale. The Writers Circle at Northern District meets on the first and third Mondays. Bring pages when you're ready, or just listen until you are. (The Leaside group is full and keeping a waitlist, so check before you go.)

## If you write a specific genre

**[Toronto SFF Writers](https://www.torontosffwriters.com/)** is for sci-fi, fantasy and horror writers. It's a volunteer-run collective that started in 2015, with weekly in-person critique sessions (in High Park through the summer), Sunday writing sprints in person and online, virtual readings and feedback nights, and a monthly pub night. Events are free. Nobody in this group will ask why your book needs a map.

**[Toronto Romance Writers](https://www.torontoromancewriters.com/)** is the city's romance chapter, with published and unpublished members from Toronto, the GTA and beyond. It runs monthly meetings with workshops and feedback, plus virtual write-ins. Guests are welcome at most meetings, so you can try one before you join.

## If you want a long-running community

Some writers want more than a weekly session. They want an organisation that will still be there in five years.

**[Mississauga Writers Group](https://mississaugawritersgroup.ca/)** is the GTA option: monthly meetings at Mississauga Central Library, workshops, and a member anthology that gives everyone something to aim at. It's a good fit if you're west of the city and don't want to cross it for a critique.

**[Writers Collective of Canada](https://wcc-cec.org/)** is the other: a workshop organisation rather than a critique circle. Its community writing workshops run at Toronto Public Library branches and online, and the format is deliberately gentle, write together, share a first draft if you feel like it, listen. Register ahead; the calendar fills.

## If you're a poet

Writing groups and poetry nights overlap more than people think. A lot of poets write alone and then read out loud.

**[Toronto Poetry Slam](https://torontopoetryslam.ca/)** has been running since 2005 and is one of the largest spoken word events in Canada: a two-round competition judged by the audience, plus workshops and the occasional festival slot. It's the competitive room, and a good place to watch before you sign up. For something quieter, Toronto Public Library branches run poetry readings and workshops through the year, and the directory's [poetry and spoken word page](/poetry-spoken-word/) lists open mics and slams across the city.

## If none of these fit

The library's [Book Clubs & Writers' Groups calendar](https://www.torontopubliclibrary.ca/programs-and-classes/categories/book-clubs-writers-groups.jsp) lists every branch group and workshop in one place, including the small ones that don't have much of a web presence. Between that and the groups above, you'll find a circle that meets at a time you can actually make.

And if nothing fits, [start your own group](/blog/start-a-group/). The library will often give you a room, and the directory will list it.

## How to stick with one

The advice here is the same as for any group. Go three times before you decide. The first session you'll feel like a stranger with a laptop. By the third, someone will ask how the chapter is going. That question is half the reason to go.

You don't need to be "a real writer" to join any of these. Writing is the qualification.
